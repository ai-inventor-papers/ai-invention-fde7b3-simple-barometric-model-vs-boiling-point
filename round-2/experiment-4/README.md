## Layman Summary

Checks a simple altitude formula for water's boiling point against a computed reference table, and separates how much of the error comes from the pressure model versus the thermodynamic assumptions.

## Full Summary

This artifact attributes the residual of a barometric boiling-point model of water to two causes. It crosses two pressure models (isothermal and ISA troposphere) with four thermodynamic variants: constant enthalpy anchored at 373.124 K, constant enthalpy anchored at 373.15 K, an effective constant enthalpy fitted to IAPWS-IF97 saturation, and direct IAPWS-IF97 saturation inversion. Residuals are taken against two computed reference series from the dataset dependency: the Engineering ToolBox table (method not stated) and IAPWS-IF97 saturation at ISA pressures. No measured boiling points are used, so every result is an agreement statistic against a computed reference, not an accuracy claim against measurement. Per-altitude residuals and band statistics (n, bias, MAE, max absolute error) are reported for 0-3, 3-6 and 6-9 km (the 6-9 km band includes 9144 m). Headline values for 6-9 km against the ToolBox table are: isothermal bias +0.47 to +1.10 K, ISA bias -1.03 to -1.72 K, depending on variant. The anchor shift (373.124 vs 373.15 K) is about -0.023 K, a minor term. The enthalpy treatment removes about 37-40% of the ISA 6-9 km bias, and increases the isothermal bias. The atmosphere effect (ISA minus isothermal) is about -2.2 K at 6-9 km and dominates the residual; no sign change occurs over 0-9144 m. Implementation checks (sea-level inversion within 3e-4 K, ISA reproduction of the IAPWS reference within 5.3e-4 K, plain-loop statistics matching to 1e-9 K) pass. An independent re-derivation from the raw residual rows reproduces the headline numbers. Limitations: the effective enthalpy is fitted to the same IAPWS curve as the reference, so it is not independent; the result describes the standard atmosphere only; and the dependency contains no measured rows.
