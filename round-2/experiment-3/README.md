## Full Summary

This artifact tests a barometric plus constant-enthalpy model of water's boiling point against measured boiling temperatures paired with station pressure, focusing on altitudes above 5 km. It implements a 2x2 set of variants: an isothermal or ISA (standard atmosphere) pressure path, crossed with a constant-enthalpy Clausius-Clapeyron or IAPWS-97 saturation thermodynamic model. Unit checks pass: the Clausius-Clapeyron and IAPWS-97 models give 373.124 K at 101325 Pa, the ISA altitude inversion round-trips to 1e-11 m, and the ISA pressure at 11 km is 22632.6 Pa. A bounded search of two query rounds (Osborne, Stimson and Ginnings 1939; high-altitude station and summit records; Keene 1995) found the NIST RP1229 paper but no transcribable measured boiling-temperature-versus-pressure rows, none above 5 km, and no retrievable Keene 1995 text. The measured set is therefore empty (n = 0) for every band, and no residual statistics are reported. This is a null outcome, not a finding: the question of how well the barometric model predicts measured boiling points at altitude is untested. Computed checks, labelled as not measured evidence, were run on the reference dataset of the upstream artifact art_39-uxNptYM5-. The IAPWS-97 variant at ISA pressures reproduces the stored computed rows to 0.00094 K. The Engineering ToolBox table, of unstated method, differs from the models by a bias of +0.37 K for the isothermal Clausius-Clapeyron variant, -0.65 K for the ISA Clausius-Clapeyron variant, and -0.30 K for the ISA IAPWS variant. Anchor sensitivity (373.15 K) shifts the Clausius-Clapeyron variants by about +0.02 K, and a plus or minus 500 J/mol change in the enthalpy of vaporization by about 0.17 K. An independent re-derivation through a separate code path (IAPWS-97 backward equation P to T, inline ISA pressure, raw JSON rows) reproduced all headline numbers. Deviations from the plan: the IAPWS solve bracket was set to 273.16 to 647.0 K, because the plan's upper bound of 373.946 K is not the critical point and does not bracket the saturation pressure. Limitations: no measured comparison was performed at any altitude, and no uncertainty intervals are given because no residuals exist.
