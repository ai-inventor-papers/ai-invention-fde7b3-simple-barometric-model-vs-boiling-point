## Full Summary

Experiment artifact for the question: how well does the simple barometric formula predict the boiling point of water at altitude, compared with published measurements? The plan transcribes measured boiling-temperature and pressure pairs from Osborne, Stimson and Ginnings (1939) and the CRC Handbook, then compares four model variants (isothermal or ISA pressure, each inverted to boiling temperature by a constant-enthalpy Clausius-Clapeyron step or by IAPWS-IF97 saturation) by altitude band. In this round the measured set is EMPTY: the NIST RP1229 PDF returned no readable table text, the CRC Handbook table was not obtained, and Keene (1995) was not located. No row was padded from computed tables. The output is therefore labelled untested, with the statement: no measured comparison; no accuracy claim; computed reference shown only as context. The computed context uses the 250-row reference table (125 Engineering ToolBox rows and 125 IAPWS-IF97 at ISA pressure rows), which is not measured data. Unit checks pass: IAPWS-IF97 at 101.325 kPa gives 373.1243 K, the ISA and isothermal pressure formulas return their expected values, and all four variants agree with 373.124 K at sea-level pressure within 0.05 K. On the computed reference, the constant-enthalpy ISA variant (V3) runs about 1.7 K below the ToolBox values at 6 km and above (n = 42, bias -1.72 K; independently re-derived as -1.716 K, and a shuffled-label placebo does not reproduce it). Moving the anchor from 373.124 K to 373.15 K shifts V1 and V3 by about +0.024 K. Outputs are full, mini and preview JSON files, band statistics, per-row residuals, a source log with zero rows retained, and reproducibility notes with pinned dependencies. The high-altitude question (6 to 9 km) remains unanswered because no measured rows exist there.
