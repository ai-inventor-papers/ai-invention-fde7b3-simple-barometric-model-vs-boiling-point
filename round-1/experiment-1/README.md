## Layman Summary

We compute how water's boiling point drops with altitude using four simple air-pressure and steam models, and compare each with a published table. The simplest shortcut stays closest; no measured data could be verified.

## Full Summary

This artifact computes the boiling point of water from 0 to 9 km in a 2x2 factorial design: atmosphere model (isothermal barometric formula versus ISA lapse-rate) crossed with thermodynamic model (constant-enthalpy Clausius-Clapeyron anchored at 373.15 K versus the IAPWS-IF97 saturation curve). Residuals (model minus the Engineering ToolBox table, in K) are reported by band (0-3, 3-6, 6-9 km) as bias, mean absolute error and maximum absolute error. Against the table, the isothermal and constant-enthalpy variant (V1) has bias +0.23, +0.51 and +0.50 K across the three bands and stays within 0.6 K everywhere. The ISA variant with constant enthalpy (V3) drifts to -1.64 K bias in the 6-9 km band, with a maximum error of 2.6 K. The two residual differences V1-V3 and V2-V4 never change sign over 0-9 km, so no crossover altitude exists. The V1-V2 difference grows with altitude, consistent with the expected behaviour of the constant-enthalpy shortcut. Sensitivity runs vary the enthalpy by ±500 J/mol and sea-level pressure by ±1 kPa. The IAPWS-IF97 normal boiling point is 373.124 K (99.974 °C); the constant-enthalpy formula gives 100.00 °C at sea level. No independently measured boiling-point and station-pressure pairs could be verified, so the measured comparison is empty and no conclusion is drawn about accuracy against measurement; the Engineering ToolBox table is a reference curve of unstated method, not a measurement. An independent re-derivation of all band statistics matches the stored values to 2.5e-13 K.
