## Layman Summary

Checks whether a popular table of water's boiling point by altitude was measured or calculated, and how a simple pressure model compares. The table drifts from the model above about 4.5 km; no independent measurements were found to settle it.

## Full Summary

This round tested the Engineering ToolBox water-boiling-point-versus-altitude table. The page states no method, formula, steam table or measured dataset, so its provenance is unknown [1]. Its sea-level and altitude rows (for example 100.0 C at 0 m and 71.1 C at 9144 m) were relayed by a summarising fetch and must be checked against the page. Using the standard ISA pressure curve and a constant-enthalpy Clausius-Clapeyron model (40.65 kJ/mol anchored at 373.124 K), the table agrees with the model to within about 0.35 K up to 4.6 km. Above that the table runs higher: about +0.8 K at 6.1 km, +1.7 K at 7.6 km, +2.6 K at 8.8 km and +2.8 K at 9.1 km. This residual pattern is a computed result from the relayed values, not a published finding. It is consistent with a table built on a different thermodynamic or pressure assumption, but it does not identify which. No independent measured boiling-point dataset with station pressure was found, so the question of how well the barometric model predicts measurements is not answered. Summit estimates in the sources cluster at 68 to 72 C [4][5], and these are calculations, not observations. Keene (1995) in the Journal of Chemical Education was not located after two rounds of search. The only related altitude-boiling article found (ACS DOI 10.1021/ed067p45) returned HTTP 403, so its content is unverified [2]. The IAPWS-95 saturation table in NIST NISTIR 5078 could not be parsed by the fetch tool, so the IAPWS reference step is incomplete. Pressure-boiling values in the Wikipedia vapour-pressure article are tied to a Wagner-Pruss/IAPWS-based correlation, and its 100 C row corresponds to about 101.4 kPa [3]. Confidence: high that the ToolBox page states no method; moderate on the residual pattern (depends on transcription); low on any claim about measurement agreement, because none was found. What would change this: a readable IAPWS saturation table to compare with, and at least two measured station-pressure datasets reaching above 8 km.
