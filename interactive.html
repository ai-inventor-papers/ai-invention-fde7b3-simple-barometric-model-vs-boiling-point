The finding

Question: how well does the simple barometric formula predict the boiling point of water at altitude, compared with published measurements?

It has not been tested against measurements, because no measured boiling-point and station-pressure pairs could be recovered. Against the Engineering ToolBox table, a , the isothermal model with a constant stays within 0.6 K at every recorded altitude. The atmosphere model matters more: above 6 km the ISA model sits about 2.1 K below the isothermal one.

    How it works

    Question: how does one altitude become a boiling temperature and then a residual?

    The stepper follows one recorded altitude, 8,992 m, the row with the largest ISA gap. Pressure at that altitude is computed in the page from the run's own constants. The boiling temperatures, the table value and the residuals are the values the run recorded. Use the buttons, the stage tabs or the left and right arrow keys.

          Try it

          Question: what does the constant-enthalpy calculation give at any altitude, and does the page reproduce the run?

          This is a live copy of the two constant-enthalpy calculations. The pressure formulas and the Clausius-Clapeyron inversion are the run's own, with the constants recorded in the run. The IAPWS values need the full steam formulation and are not recomputed here; they stay as recorded.

          Where it fails

          Question: at what size of miss does each combination leave the table, and where does that happen?

          The counts use the recorded residuals, rounded to four decimals in the run's file. A residual that sits exactly on the threshold may fall on either side.

          What the paper says it does not show

          • No measured comparison. Zero measured boiling-point and station-pressure pairs were retained, so nothing here tests accuracy against measurement.
          • Unstated reference method. The Engineering ToolBox table's method is unknown, and above 5 km it agrees with none of the four combinations.
          • Shared reference. The reference series rest on the same IAPWS saturation curve, so the fitted enthalpy is not independent of the reference.
          • Standard atmosphere only. The ISA residuals describe a standard atmosphere, not weather or local conditions at a station.
          • No uncertainty estimates. The computations are deterministic, so no interval is given.
