Isothermal Barometric Boiling Points Match a Computed Table to 0.6 K

We asked how well the barometric formula, combined with the Clausius-Clapeyron equation, predicts the boiling temperature of water at altitude. No measured boiling-point and station-pressure pairs could be recovered, so the test runs against the Engineering ToolBox table instead. The isothermal model with a constant enthalpy of vaporisation stays within 0.6 K of that table from 0 to 9.1 km. This is one exploratory estimate against a computed reference, not an accuracy result against measurement. The choice of atmosphere model matters more: above 6 km the ISA version sits about 2.1 K below the isothermal one.

Contributions

  • Four model combinations compared

    Isothermal and ISA pressure profiles, each inverted to a boiling temperature by constant-enthalpy and IAPWS models, tested over 0 to 9.1 km.

  • Atmosphere choice dominates

    Switching from the isothermal to the ISA pressure model moves the constant-enthalpy prediction by about 2.1 K at 6 to 9 km, more than the enthalpy approximation does.

  • A documented null result

    No measured pairs above 5 km were available, so the measured criteria could not be evaluated and the study makes no accuracy claim against measurement.

Method

Each prediction takes an altitude in and returns a boiling temperature out, through two stages. The first stage converts altitude to pressure with one of two atmosphere models. The isothermal model uses a single exponential scale height for the whole range. The ISA model adds a troposphere with a fixed lapse rate of 6.5 K per km. The second stage converts pressure to boiling temperature with one of two thermodynamic models. The constant-enthalpy model applies the Clausius-Clapeyron equation with an enthalpy of vaporisation of 40.65 kJ/mol, anchored at 373.15 K and 101,325 Pa. The IAPWS model inverts the IAPWS saturation curve by numerical root-finding.

Two stages with two options each give four combinations. Each combination is then compared against the Engineering ToolBox table at the same altitudes. The residual is model minus table, in kelvin. The table's method is not stated, so the table serves as a computed reference, not a measurement.

The reference set holds 250 rows, 125 from each of two sources, and no measured rows. The first computation used 119 altitudes from 0 to 9,144 m. The two later computations used 125 altitudes from -305 m to 9,144 m. Bands are 0 to 3 km, 3 to 6 km and 6 km and above.

The study planned to compare the models with measured boiling temperatures from Osborne, Stimson and Ginnings and from a CRC Handbook table. Neither source yielded a transcribable table, and the NIST RP1229 document could not be parsed. Zero measured pairs were retained.

Dataset check and sensitivity

Across 125 paired altitudes, the Engineering ToolBox table differs from the ISA saturation reference by -0.31 K to +1.94 K, with a median of +0.02 K. Below 5 km the table lies within -0.31 K to +0.11 K of that reference, so it tracks an ISA calculation closely at low altitude. Moving the anchor from 373.124 K to 373.15 K shifts the constant-enthalpy predictions by about +0.02 K. Changing the enthalpy of vaporisation by 500 J/mol changes them by about 0.17 K.

What the choice of model changes

The atmosphere model moves the predicted boiling temperature by about 2.1 K at 6 to 9 km. The enthalpy approximation moves it by roughly 0.5 to 0.6 K there and by less than 0.1 K below 3 km. Replacing constant enthalpy with the IAPWS model in the ISA version removes 37 to 40 percent of its 6 to 9 km bias.

Limitations

  • No measured comparison. Zero measured boiling-point and station-pressure pairs were retained, and no measured band reached the minimum of three samples needed for a statistic. The measured criteria could not be evaluated, and nothing here tests accuracy against measurement.
  • Unstated reference method. The Engineering ToolBox table's method is unknown. Its method above 5 km is unidentified, since it agrees with none of the four variants there. Its values were transcribed from a summary of the page and were not checked against the source page in this study.
  • Shared reference. Both reference series rest on the same IAPWS saturation curve, so the effective enthalpy fitted to that curve is not independent of the reference.
  • Standard atmosphere only. The ISA residuals describe a standard atmosphere. A real station departs from it with weather and local conditions.
  • No uncertainty estimates. The computations are deterministic and give no sampling variance or interval. The two later computations use the same table, so their agreement confirms arithmetic, not the table.
  • Gaps in the search. Keene (1995) was not located. An ACS article on boiling at altitude (DOI 10.1021/ed067p45) could not be retrieved, so its content is unverified. The IAPWS-95 saturation table in NIST NISTIR 5078 could not be parsed, so that reference check is incomplete.

The paper's own next step is a measured comparison: boiling temperatures paired with station pressure above 6 km, with each atmosphere and thermodynamic model stated in advance.
